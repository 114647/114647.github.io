Now that I have finished the story and have some time to look deeper into it, I finally understand a bit more about the symbolism and themes that Doris Lessing uses. I admit, this part took me the most effort, because understanding symbolism in a foreign language is not always easy. But once I started connecting the events in the story with the bigger ideas behind them, it all became more meaningful. One of the strongest themes is the transition from childhood to maturity. In the beginning, David believes he is invincible, he thinks he can control everything, even his own pain. But when he sees the suffering and death of the injured buck, that illusion is shattered. The dying buck is, in my opinion, the most symbolic moment in the story. It is the reality of life and death, something David cannot escape no matter how strong or confident he feels. The veld, with its beauty and danger, almost acts as a metaphor for life itself. At first it seems simple, something he can conquer, but then it becomes clear that nature is far bigger and more powerful than he imagined. This realization forces him to confront the idea that he, too, is mortal. I think this sudden awareness is what truly marks his growth, even if it comes in a painful way.
